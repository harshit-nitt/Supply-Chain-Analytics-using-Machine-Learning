      Recommended actions

      Ordered by expected impact.

      • Reset promised delivery windows

        First Class and Second Class orders miss their promise 95% and 77% of the time. Recalibrate the scheduled days for these tiers or renegotiate carrier SLAs.

      • Use the model as a triage flag

        Send high-risk orders to an operations review queue to expedite, re-route or reset customer expectations. Do not auto-block orders.

      • Validate two unexpected signals

        Confirm with the payments team why transfer payments matter, and confirm that processing and pending statuses are known at order time.

      • Prioritise by risk, not margin

        Late and on-time orders earn almost the same profit ($31.42 vs $31.69 median), so rank interventions by delay probability and customer impact.

      How it was built

      Columns only known after delivery were removed first, so the model sees only what exists when an order is placed.

      1. CleanRemoved leakage, constant and ID columns; capped price outliers.
      2. Engineer13 features including lead time, seasonality and delay-rate encodings fit on training data only.
      3. CompareLogistic Regression, Decision Tree and Random Forest on identical data.
      4. TuneRandomizedSearchCV on the best model, scored by ROC-AUC.
      5. ExplainPermutation importance and partial dependence plots.

      Data: DataCo Smart Supply Chain dataset, 180,519 order lines, January 2015 to September 2017.

      Built with Python, pandas and scikit-learn. All figures come from the project's held-out test results.
